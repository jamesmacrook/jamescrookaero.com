About

Eastbourne → Cambridge → Oxfordshire → California

I grew up in Eastbourne and started the way a lot of engineers do, building a Locost sports car. Formula Student and an engineering degree at Cambridge took me into Formula 1, where I spent about ten years in Oxfordshire working on aerodynamics with Lotus and then Williams.

In 2022 I moved to Tesla as Staff Aerodynamicist and led aerodynamic development of the next-generation Roadster, including its deployable active rear wing, on which I'm a named inventor.

Now I work with Navier AI on fast, AI-driven aerodynamic simulation, and help early-stage hardware teams with their aero: aircraft, boats, bikes, cars and more.

Formula 1

Lotus F1 Team · Williams F1 · Oxfordshire

About ten years of Formula 1 aerodynamics: CFD, wind-tunnel testing and track correlation, first at Lotus in Enstone and then at Williams in Grove.

One favourite: the curved wing mirrors on the 2019 Williams FW42. They turned heads at pre-season testing in Barcelona, and the FIA wasn't happy with them, so they were swapped out before the first race in Melbourne.

Tune to Wing

Formula SAE aerodynamics series

Tune to Wing is my video series on race-car aerodynamics for Formula SAE teams: designing a wing from first principles, reading a pressure plot, and breaking down a CFD result the way an F1 aero department would.

This year I took it live at FSAE Electric at Michigan International Speedway, where I was also a design judge, and gave the talk to a room full of student teams.

Locost

Where it started · Eastbourne

At 18 I built a Locost, following Ron Champion's book Build Your Own Sports Car for as Little as £250: a home-made take on the Lotus Seven, built around a welded steel spaceframe.

Most of it I made myself, with key components from Caterham, specialist suppliers and Ford.
